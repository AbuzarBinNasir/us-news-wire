The intensifying military conflict between the United States and Iran has moved beyond a regional skirmish, directly impacting the domestic economy and global maritime stability. Recent drone and missile exchanges have expanded into new territories, including Egypt and Jordan, contributing to a noticeable cooling of the U.S. economy. While consumer spending remains resilient, the Commerce Department’s report of a 1.5 percent GDP growth rate suggests that the uncertainty of war is beginning to weigh on broader expansion. This volatility is perhaps felt most acutely by American households through the housing market, where mortgage rates have climbed to a one-year high of 6.66 percent. As shipping risks in the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz drive up energy costs and fuel inflation fears, the Federal Reserve faces a complicated path toward stabilizing prices without further stifling growth.

Within Washington, the administration’s efforts to solidify its leadership at the Department of Justice have hit a significant roadblock. The nomination of Todd Blanche for Attorney General is currently in limbo as key Republican senators, including John Cornyn and Thom Tillis, withhold their support over the funding of specific DOJ programs. This internal party friction highlights a growing debate over the "weaponization" of federal agencies and the degree of autonomy the executive branch should maintain over law enforcement priorities.
